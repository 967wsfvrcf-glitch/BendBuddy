Quick field guide

Common EMT rules of thumb
  • Offset: spacing = rise × multiplier.
  • 10°: multiplier 5.76; approximate shrink 1/16 in per inch of rise.
  • 22.5°: multiplier 2.6; approximate shrink 3/16 in per inch of rise.
  • 30°: multiplier 2; approximate shrink 1/4 in per inch of rise.
  • 45°: multiplier 1.4; approximate shrink 3/8 in per inch of rise.
  • 60°: multiplier 1.155; approximate shrink 1/2 in per inch of rise.
  • 90° stub: mark = desired stub height − bender take-up.

These are approximate field formulas. Follow the bender's markings, plans, applicable code, and employer procedures. Verify before cutting or bending.

Spacing ≈ rise × multiplier. Approximate multipliers:

Example: a 2-inch rise at 30° gives about 4 inches between marks.

Shrink depends on bend angle and rise; confirm the appropriate multiplier and bender guidance.

90° stub-up reminder

  1. Choose the required stub height.
  2. Find the take-up stamped on the correct bender or in its instructions.
  3. Mark the conduit: desired height − take-up.
  4. Align the mark with the bender's arrow and bend using the bender instructions.

Take-up varies by bender and conduit size. Do not rely on a universal take-up number.

Conduit fill: check before pulling

  • Count all conductors/cables and identify their insulation and sizes.
  • Use the adopted code's raceway-fill tables and rules for the exact raceway type and conductor combination.
  • Account for equipment grounding conductors, cable assemblies, nipples, and other applicable rules.
  • Check conductor ampacity adjustment and terminal temperature limits separately from fill.

Do not guess fill from appearance. Use the applicable code tables or an approved calculator.

Wire size & breaker check

  • Confirm load, continuous-load requirements, conductor material, insulation rating, ambient temperature, and number of current-carrying conductors.
  • Verify overcurrent protection, terminal temperature ratings, and any equipment-specific instructions.
  • Check voltage drop for long runs where applicable.
  • Never choose wire size from breaker size alone.

Use the locally adopted electrical code and approved design; this sheet intentionally does not prescribe a universal wire size.

Box fill: count carefully

  • Identify the box's marked volume or listed volume.
  • Count insulated conductors, device yokes, internal clamps, and equipment grounding conductors using the applicable code rules.
  • Use the correct volume allowance for the conductor size.
  • Confirm the box is listed for the installation and has enough room for safe terminations.

Box-fill calculations are code-specific. Use the current adopted rules and listed box volume.

Before working on electrical equipment

  1. Identify all sources, including backfeeds, stored energy, and alternate supplies.
  2. Follow the site's lockout/tagout procedure and apply your personal lock as required.
  3. Use a properly rated tester and verify it on a known live source, test for absence of voltage, then re-verify the tester.
  4. Use the required PPE and boundaries based on the risk assessment.

Never assume equipment is de-energized. Only qualified persons should perform electrical work, following applicable rules and training.

Jobsite quick safety scan

  • Look for overhead lines, wet conditions, damaged cords, sharp edges, and trip hazards.
  • Inspect tools, leads, ladders, and PPE before use.
  • Keep ladders and long conduit away from power lines; follow required clearances.
  • In hot conditions, take breaks, cool down, and follow the site's hydration and heat-safety plan.

Stop and ask the supervisor when the hazard or safe procedure is unclear.

Dead circuit: safe first checks

  • Ask what changed and identify the affected equipment and circuit.
  • Check drawings, labels, and upstream devices without opening energized equipment unless qualified and authorized.
  • Use a properly rated meter and safe work procedures to verify conditions.
  • Check for tripped protective devices only when safe; investigate why a breaker or GFCI tripped before resetting repeatedly.

Do not bypass protective devices or repeatedly reset a device that trips again.

GFCI keeps tripping

  • Unplug connected loads if safe and permitted.
  • Look for moisture, damaged cords, or equipment faults.
  • Do not defeat the GFCI or replace it with a non-protective device.
  • If it trips with loads disconnected or will not reset, stop and investigate per approved procedures.

EMT installation reminders

  • Use fittings listed for the raceway and environment.
  • Ream cut ends and protect conductors from sharp edges.
  • Support and secure raceway according to the adopted code and installation conditions.
  • Check bonding/grounding continuity and fitting tightness as required.

Exact support spacing and exceptions depend on the adopted code and installation.

Field verification checklist

  • ☐ Confirm the obstruction and required clearance.
  • ☐ Measure from a consistent reference point.
  • ☐ Verify angle, multiplier, and take-up against the actual bender.
